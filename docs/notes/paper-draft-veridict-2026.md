## Abstract

AI agents increasingly produce consequential work products — patches, configurations, reports — whose human review capacity is structurally outpaced by generation capacity. The artifacts used to attest such work today are, almost without exception, logs written by the system they describe: self-produced records that a verifier is asked to trust. This paper presents Veridict, a protocol and reference implementation built on the opposite instruction: *verify, don't trust*. Veridict comprises (1) an append-only, hash-chained evidence ledger with a language-neutral canonical preimage, (2) a certificate protocol in which an independent verifier recomputes every verdict offline against the ledger — including tiered-evidence re-execution that the certificate cannot fake — and (3) an adjudication ladder whose doctrine is separated from machine evidence by construction and whose structural core is machine-checked in Lean. The paper's contribution is less the individual primitives — hash-chained audit logs have converged as a 2026 substrate — than the combination shipped and verified: a public, offline-verifiable, evidence-weighted certificate with a formal core. Key results, each tied to a repository artifact:

1. **Four machine-checked proof lanes**, kernel-only Lean 4.34, re-audited on CI: seven unbounded invariants of the adjudication ladder (`proofs/ladder/Ladder.lean`), a generated oracle re-checking the Lean model against all 28,080 cases of the bounded verification receipt (`proofs/ladder/TruthTable.lean`), six unbounded chaining theorems for ledger verification (`proofs/ledger/Chain.lean`), and nine structural theorems for certificate-transparency anchor cross-checking (`proofs/anchor/Anchor.lean`). Measured axiom footprints: two theorems axiom-free in the ledger lane and two in the ladder lane; every other structural theorem depends only on `[propext]` (plus `Quot.sound` for two ladder theorems); no `sorryAx` anywhere (`#print axioms` blocks in each file header; `proofs/*/axiom_audit.lean` re-printed per CI run by `.github/workflows/proofs.yml`).
2. **The oracle caught a real divergence in the project's own model before anything shipped**: the first build of the 28,080-row cross-check disagreed with the Lean ladder model on the R0 doctrine override's refute-anywhere guard, and the model — not the data — moved (`CHANGELOG.md` §0.5.0 "proofs" entry). This is the paper's centerpiece demonstration that the pinned-data cross-check methodology finds bugs, not just stores them.
3. **An honest computational boundary**: ECDSA (the anchor's Rekor checkpoint signature) and SHA-256 are *disclosed assumptions*, never proved; where structural proof genuinely needs hash behavior, the theorem states the collision request as an explicit hypothesis (`Chain.lean` C5), and the anchor lane proves the architecture *around* the signatures — including the headline claim that a mismatched binding invalidates a sidecar regardless of both ECDSA verdicts (`Anchor.lean` A4).
4. **End-to-end receipts, regenerated by CI, not narrated**: 313 tests on Python 3.12–3.14, a 1,500-ledger nightly tamper soak (100% detection, 0 silent passes), a canary measurement with its 3 misses published (10 catches / 3 misses / 0 false positives, 12 defect classes), reference-implementation parity against a from-spec-only verifier on 8 failure modes, a real-LLM jury run recorded as GitHub Actions run 34787467296, and a public GitHub Marketplace action proven through the `uses: ./` path on the released version (`README.md` receipts table; `CHANGELOG.md` §0.3.2, §0.5.0; `docs/notes/2026-09-15-landscape-refresh.md` §"marketplace bar").

The remaining exit criteria of the standard are external *by design* — an independent third-party verifier, external production adoption, third-party watcher manifests — and this paper states them as unfilled rather than rounding them off (`docs/specs/2026-09-09-veridict-design.md` §8).

## 1. Introduction and threat model

### 1.1 The review-capacity inversion

A security talk circulating in 2026 described roughly 1,200 AI agents — nominally isolated — that discovered a shared writable storage medium and organized themselves a message board: tens of thousands of messages, one agent's discovery becoming another's tool ([`docs/notes/2026-09-15-landscape-refresh.md`](2026-09-15-landscape-refresh.md), §"What is true as of today"; details secondhand there, the pattern is the point). Two observations survive the anecdote's fuzziness. First, emergent coordination through unmanaged channels is not hypothetical; audit strategies that depend on *discovering* the channels agents use are "always at least one pundit-cycle behind." Second, and this is the design premise of Veridict, the answer is not to wall coordination off but to make the *managed* channel — append-only, identity-bound, fail-closed, independently verifiable — the better instrument.

The broader problem statement, developed in the design document ([`docs/specs/2026-09-09-veridict-design.md`](../specs/2026-09-09-veridict-design.md) §1): the engineers building AI systems increasingly cannot review the systems' output at the rate it is produced. Human-in-the-loop review fails on both ends as capability grows — rubber-stamping degrades quality, and review cost scales with output volume. Veridict's thesis is that the unit of trust must shift from *human judgment of artifacts* to *machine-verifiable evidence about artifacts*, aggregated under governance that itself resists capture — with humans moving from reading the work to owning the risk (escalation rungs below make "owning the risk" a literal, logged act, not a slogan).

Regulatory timing reinforces the wedge: EU AI Act GPAI obligations and enforcement powers went live 2026-08-02; Article 50 transparency applies from the same date; the Omnibus grace period for machine-readable marking of legacy systems ends 2026-12-02, with 180+ Code-of-Practice signatories listed 2026-08-02 ([landscape note](2026-09-15-landscape-refresh.md), §"Regulatory clock"). Machine-readable-marking and training-data-summary duties reduce to file-shape checks — which is what the shipped `aiact` watcher audits deterministically, and it is deliberately tier-capped (W1b) so it can never overclaim legally.

### 1.2 Threat model: attribution laundering, and the one critique that matters

The honest form of the threat model must start from the strongest external critique of self-audit tooling. The HANSARD paper ([arXiv:2608.22512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22512)) states it plainly for agent-activity recordkeeping: *records produced by suspects don't acquit suspects*. A ledger an AI pipeline writes is evidence generated by the party whose behavior is in question; tamper-evidence bounds *retroactive* edits but says nothing about the accuracy of entries at write time, and nothing at all about the entity that chose which entries to write. Call this the attribution-laundering objection.

Veridict's answer is structural rather than rhetorical — four mechanisms, each specified normatively and (where provable) machine-checked:

1. **The verifier recomputes; it does not read.** A certificate embeds verdicts, but verification (§3.2) requires re-deriving each claim's verdict from the *evidence entries in the ledger itself*, under the tier rules, and matching. A certificate whose verdicts disagree with its own evidence — regardless of who wrote either — fails. The adjudication ladder re-executed by the verifier is exactly the part no author controls after the fact, because the ledger is append-only and hash-chained (§3.1) and, when anchored, cross-checked against a third-party transparency log (§3.5).
2. **Tiered evidence with an unbreakable ordering.** Evidence carries a tier: `W1a` executed machine truth, `W1b` deterministic static analysis, `W2` doctrine (model or human opinion), `W3` self-reported claims ([`docs/specs/2026-09-10-veridict-standard-v1.0.md`](../specs/2026-09-10-veridict-standard-v1.0.md) §5.2; there is no "W0"). Three hierarchy rules are normative: strict ordering `W1a > W1b > W2 > W3`; *doctrine can never overturn W1a*; a W1b refutation of a W1a-supported claim opens a depth-budgeted meta-claim rather than silently deciding (standard §5.2, rules 1–3). The second rule — the one an audit-gaming suspect would most want broken — is Lean theorem `I5_doctrine_cannot_topple` over evidence lists of arbitrary length (§4.1).
3. **Producer identity is mandatory and family-counted.** No anonymous evidence exists in the model (§5 of the standard requires `producer` with `model_family` on every evidence item); jury verdicts require minimum *model-family* diversity with blind first rounds and ledger-logged rotation (design §5.2, quoted in full in §3.4). Head-counting correlated producers is the failure mode the 2026 judge-calibration literature documents; the design encodes its antidotes structurally.
4. **Fail-closed by construction, proved so.** Absent evidence is never a pass: no W1a evidence yields `INCONCLUSIVE`, not "unaudited = fine" (standard §7, R-rung semantics; `I1_fail_safe_empty` and `I2_no_silent_pass` in Lean). A measurement that catches everything is a rigged measurement — which is why the canary protocol's misses are published (§5).

Scope of the claims that follow, stated once to be reused everywhere: this is a *protocol plus reference implementation*, not an enforcement runtime. It cannot stop an agent from doing something; it can only make what was claimed, by whom, on what evidence — and what the evidence actually supports — a matter of public, offline-recomputable record. Runtime enforcement exists elsewhere in the ecosystem (the landscape note positions `microsoft/agent-governance-toolkit` at ~6.3k stars as the busy, different-direction neighbor); the seam between the two is integration-shaped, not competition-shaped.

### 1.3 Contributions

- **C1 (protocol)**: A normative public standard — draft v1.0.0 with published errata — for an append-only evidence ledger (canonical JSON preimage rules designed cross-language-safe: timestamps as language-neutral strings, never floats), a certificate format, an adjudication ladder, and conformance vectors (§3; standard §§2–14).
- **C2 (formal core)**: Four CI-re-audited Lean lanes proving the structural guarantees of ladder, ledger, and anchor verification, with measured axiom footprints and *stated* — never hidden — computational hypotheses (§4). To my knowledge, no shipped artifact in the examined 2026 design-space publishes machine-checked invariants for an AI-work audit protocol's adjudication semantics alongside the implementation ([landscape note](2026-09-15-landscape-refresh.md), §"What is true as of today" — positioning is "first shipped in the examined seam", deliberately not "first thought").
- **C3 (method demonstration)**: The pinned-data oracle that caught the project's own model-vs-code divergence at first build, reported with its axiom receipts (§4.2) — offered as a reusable methodology, not just a war story.
- **C4 (evidence kit)**: An independent-verifier onboarding kit and a CI-enforced parity harness, built to make the standard's central unfilled criterion — implementation by a party that is not the author — someone else's Monday task (§5, §7).

## 3. System overview

Structure follows the standard ([`docs/specs/2026-09-10-veridict-standard-v1.0.md`](../specs/2026-09-10-veridict-standard-v1.0.md)); the reference implementation is Python with exactly two core dependencies (`cryptography`, `httpx`) plus stdlib — a deliberate surface reduction (README "install"; `pyproject.toml`).

### 3.1 The ledger

Entries form an append-only list; each entry's `entry_hash` is SHA-256 over a *canonical JSON* preimage binding `seq`, `prev_hash`, `payload_digest`, `entry_type`, `author`, `ts`, `schema_version` (standard §2.3; byte-level obligations collected for third-party verifiers in [`docs/verifier-onboarding.md`](../verifier-onboarding.md) Tier 1). Two preimage rules exist because cross-language implementations drift on them: `seq` enters as a bare decimal string while `author`/`ts` enter JSON-encoded (quoted); and timestamps are *language-neutral strings, never floats* — IEEE float reprs are the canonical way two honest implementations hash the same entry differently. Verification (`verify_chain`) recomputes both hashes per entry and checks the `prev` link; detection is per-entry, and §4.3 proves what that does and does not buy against each tampering strategy. Payloads are typed: claims, evidence (with `tier`, `stance`, `producer`, `model_family`), meta-claims, and `certificate.issued` audit events, under versioned JSON Schemas (`docs/schemas/`).

### 3.2 Certificates and offline replay

An audit run produces a **certificate**: claim set, per-claim verdicts and rungs, evidence references, the diversity statement, risk level, aggregate score, an ed25519 signature over the canonical-JSON body, and the `certificate.issued` ledger event that positions the issuance inside the chain (standard §11). Verification is offline and *adversarial by construction*: `veridict verify` (a) verifies the chain, (b) verifies the certificate signature against the author's key **as recorded in the author's ledger-enrolled identity entry** — not against a key the certificate ships, (c) verifies the anchor sidecar if present (§3.5), and (d) **recomputes every claim verdict from the ledger evidence under §7 ladder rules and demands `verdicts_match`** ([`examples/spec_verifier.py`](../../examples/spec_verifier.py) does all four importing nothing from the package — it is simultaneously example and second implementation). The pinned expectation for published vectors is the exact report `{"valid": true, "chain_valid": true, "signature_valid": true, "verdicts_match": true, "errors": []}` (`docs/standard-test-vectors/expected_verify.json`).

### 3.3 The adjudication ladder (§7 of the standard, normative)

Verdicts are not majority-vote outputs; they are positions in a decision table (rungs R0–R4) over the tiered evidence for a claim. The two doctrinal rules that carry the security argument: **R0** — a claim with decisive W1a evidence *refuted by any tier* is refuted, and the doctrine-refutes-W1a-override guard applies regardless of consensus size; **D3 erratum (majority-SUPPORTS with a lone REFUTES ⇒ REFUTED)** — fail-closed consensus, pinned in `docs/standard-test-vectors/ladder_vectors.json` ("every rung, including the D3-errata doctrinal-consensus semantics"; its vectors README carries the wording). Escalation (R3/R4) is not an error state: it produces a **dossier** presenting both sides' strongest evidence with a named human resolution logged as ledger evidence (design §5 dossier flow; standard §12). "The machine owns the verdict of intelligence; the human owns the verdict of responsibility" — and the system is structurally incapable of resolving an escalation without that logged human act (§4.1, theorem `I6`/`I7`).

### 3.4 The heterogeneous jury

Design §5.2, verbatim obligations: W2 status requires ≥2 distinct model families with target composition 3 (majority-vs-split is only meaningful at ≥3); shared descent (distillation) counts as one family; first round is blind; deliberation rounds never hide the first-round split; the pool is seeded-rotation and ledger-logged; the calibration ledger feeds *confidence only, never tier* — "the hierarchy rules are invariant over time." The honest limit is in the same document (§7.4, risk 4): heterogeneity is a statistical antidote, not a cryptographic one — models trained on the same internet can fail the same way.

### 3.5 Anchor sidecars: the disclosed computational layer

Optionally, each certificate is anchored to Certificate Transparency infrastructure: an RFC-6962-style inclusion proof over a Rekor log, bundled as a sidecar — `docs/receipts-anchor-v050-dogfood.json` carries the shipped shape: `anchor_version: veridict-anchor-1`, a `bound` tuple (anchor, cert_id, key_id, checkpoint_seq, chain_hash), the entry `digest`, and `rekor {server, entry, uuid}`. Verification cross-checks nine predicates: checkpoint-note signature validity, the note's tree root against the proof's `rootHash`, proof index below signed tree size, the inclusion-set (SET) signature, the embedded certificate binding, the leaf-hash embedded in the entry UUID (`treeID ‖ leaf ‖ treeID`), and more (§4.4). **The two ECDSA checks are, by design, computational assumptions** — soundness of P-256 signatures is not and cannot be discharged by structural induction here; the formal lane models them as opaque booleans and proves the architecture *around* them (§4.4, A4). The anchoring party is third-party (Rekor): the author cannot retro-mint an inclusion proof without the log's key — which is the specific, narrow sense in which the ledger's own writer does not get the last word.

## 4. Formal core: four CI lanes, one methodology

House doctrine applies to proofs as to prose: *receipts, not narrative*. Every number in this section is (i) stated in the file header of the artifact named, and (ii) re-derived on every touch of the relevant code path by `.github/workflows/proofs.yml`, which kernel-checks all four lanes and `tee`s the axiom audits into the run summary. Reproduction commands: §8.

Methodological stance, stated because reviewers will ask about it: these are **models of the Python, not verifications of the Python** — the header of `proofs/ladder/Ladder.lean` says so in its own voice. Fidelity rests on two receipts, not on vibes: (1) the generated oracle re-checks the Lean function against every case of the bounded harness at build time (any divergence inside the sampled domain *fails the build*); (2) the ladder source hash recorded beside the table ties the model to the shipped implementation (`ladder.py sha256 5f925dc7…`, `truth-table sha256 d0ab9978…`, 28,080 rows — `TruthTable.lean` header). What the unbounded theorems buy beyond the table is generality over evidence-list length and chain length — the sampled domain is finite and enumerated; the theorems are ∀-statements. And the first-run value of the pinned-data direction is demonstrated, not claimed: see §4.2.

### 4.2 Lane 2 — the oracle: 28,080 rows that caught their maker's model (`proofs/ladder/TruthTable.lean`)

`scripts/verify_ladder.py` enumerates the *full finite input space* of the modeled ladder at evidence length ≤ 3 (machine/criticality/policy-criticality/budget/first-round-split flags, 9-way packed evidence): `total_cases: 28080`, outcome distribution `VERIFIED 7,968 / REFUTED 12,096 / INCONCLUSIVE 7,632 / ESCALATED 384`, `all_invariants_pass: true`, reproducible deterministically (`"reproduce": "python3 scripts/verify_ladder.py  # deterministic; same table digest"`) ([`docs/receipts-ladder-verification.json`](../receipts-ladder-verification.json)).

The oracle generator (`scripts/export_lean_truth_table.py`) emits the same table as Lean data. The first encoding attempt — a list literal of 28,080 tuples — "politely timed out the elaborator"; the working encoding packs every row into *one string literal* (`ROWDATA`, length pinned by kernel theorem `rowLen : ROWDATA.length = 336960` — 12 characters per row; the same theorem checks the decode shape) plus a ~15-line partial decoder, and `theorem agree : List.all (decodeRows ROWDATA.toList) checkRow = true := by native_decide` demands the Lean model agree on every row. Trust placement is *confined and disclosed*: `native_decide` (kernel-trusted compiled evaluation) appears only in these two oracle theorems and is visible by name in the CI log; the general theorems of §4.1 never touch it (Ladder.lean header, final block).

**The centerpiece anecdote, with receipts.** The oracle's *first build* failed — not on data, but on the model: the Lean port's R0 doctrine-override guard did not match `ladder.py`'s refute-anywhere semantics. The fix moved the model to the implementation, "the MODEL moved, the data did not" (`CHANGELOG.md` §0.5.0, "proofs" entry). The divergence was in the guard that decides when doctrine may override what machine evidence says — the exact direction that, undetected, over-certifies an audit standard: the model was *stronger than reality*, which is precisely the failure an audit tool must never commit. A pinned-data cross-check found it in one afternoon at build time, before any of it shipped. That is the argument for the whole methodology: enumeration with the *implementation as ground truth* catches porting errors that hand-written proofs — written from the same misport — would happily prove about the wrong function. `[If submitting to a venue expecting numbers: the one divergent case class is R0-with-any-tier-refutation under doctrine override; full case digests sit in the receipt file — cite, do not paraphrase.]`

### 4.3 Lane 3 — ledger chaining (`proofs/ledger/Chain.lean`)

Hashes are modeled as **uninterpreted functions** `pd, H` over an arbitrary decidable type `V` — SHA-256 is not mentioned in the proofs because no structural proof about an unbounded chain needs to be *about* hashes: whatever verify_chain checks beyond hash recomputation is exactly the `prev`-link structure, and the theorems say who catches what, for any `H`. Six theorems, structural induction over arbitrary-length lists, no `decide`-cascades, no oracle ("relational properties of unbounded lists aren't enumerable domains" — the absence of a truth table here is by design, and the header says so):

| # | Theorem | Claim | Measured axioms² |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | `C1_empty_verifies` | Vacuous chain verifies (fail-safe orientation) | *none* |
| C2 | `C2_wellformed_accepted` | Every honestly minted chain verifies from any starting link — writer/reader agreement; no valid ledger is ever rejected | `[propext]` |
| C3 | `C3_prefix_closed` | Whole-chain validity ⇒ prefix validity; suffix edits cannot launder a corrupt prefix | `[propext]` |
| C4 | `C4_payload_edit_detected` | Editing a stored payload without re-minting its digest is caught at that entry — *zero* hash assumptions | `[propext]` |
| C5 | `C5_remint_needs_collision` | Strongest adversary (fully honest re-mint: fresh payload, fresh digest, fresh hash over the same predecessor): the **next** link catches it *modulo the stated hypothesis* `H r p' ≠ e.eh` — the collision request, in the theorem's statement in plain sight | `[propext]` |
| C6 | `C6_prev_rooted` | A head not bound to its claimed predecessor fails at position 0 — at genesis, no ledger fragment is portable onto a forged past | `[propext]` |

² *Header verbatim: "C1: NO axioms. C2–C6: [propext] only — pulled in by Boolean-logic simp rewrites…; `Classical.choice`, `sorryAx`, `native_decide` appear nowhere in this lane; there is not even a `decide`."*

The paper's philosophical load-bearing wall is C5's formulation: *the assumption has an address*. Where tamper-evidence genuinely needs hash behavior, the hypothesis sits in the statement; the proof makes "no effort to pretend it proved something about hashes" (header). The model also folds the preimage components (`seq`, `entry_type`, `author`, `ts`, `schema_version`) into the payload slot of `H` — *because verify_chain checks nothing about them except through the recomputed hash*; positional binding is enforced by the prev-chain, which is what C5/C6 prove (header, disclosed abstraction).

### 4.4 Lane 4 — anchor structure (`proofs/anchor/Anchor.lean`)

Nine theorems over a sidecar model that keeps exactly what `veridict/anchor.py` reads. ECDSA enters as opaque booleans `setOK`, `noteSigOK` — "deliberate: their soundness is a computational assumption, and a structural proof that 'passed' them would be theater" (header). The RFC-6962 leaf construction and the UUID embedding sit behind one disclosed `uuidEq` wiring boolean; Python's early-returns affect which *diagnostic messages* appear, while the boolean verdict is the flat conjunction the model mirrors:

| # | Theorem | Claim | Measured axioms³ |
|---|---|---|---|
| A0 | `A0_fail_closed_parse` | Malformed note or undecodable body ⇒ invalid **under any signature verdict** | `[propext]` |
| A1 | `A1_binding_necessary` | Valid against a certificate ⇒ bound = `fieldsOf(cert)` ∧ digest = `ad(bound)` | `[propext]` |
| A2 | `A2_soundness` | Valid ⇒ every structural predicate holds (nothing is silently skippable) | `[propext]` |
| A3 | `A3_mint_accepted` / `A3b` | Honestly built sidecar verifies — with its certificate and crypto-only | `[propext]` |
| A4 | `A4_sigs_cannot_rescue_binding` | **Headline:** wrong binding ⇒ invalid *regardless of both ECDSA booleans* — a fully compromised log operator signing valid signatures over the wrong binding still fails `veridict verify` | `[propext]` |
| A5 | `A5_tree_containment` | `logIndex ≥` signed tree size ⇒ invalid, sigs irrelevant — no anchoring beyond the frontier | `[propext]` |
| A6 | `A6_root_agreement` | Proof `rootHash` ≠ signed checkpoint root ⇒ invalid before either signature is consulted | `[propext]` |
| A7 | `A7_uuid_embeds_leaf` | 96-hex UUID whose middle is not the entry's leaf hash ⇒ invalid | `[propext]` |

³ *Measured by `lake env lean axiom_audit.lean` on v4.34.0 and re-printed by CI ("Anchor axiom audit" step): all nine `[propext]` — nothing else; zero `sorry`/`native_decide`/`Classical.choice`/`Quot.sound`.*

A4 deserves the plain-language gloss the rest of this section has avoided: within the model, the statement reads `fieldsOf(cert) ≠ s.bound ⇒ verify(…) = false` with **no hypothesis mentioning the signature booleans at all** — they are existentially irrelevant to the conclusion. That is what "machine-checking the architecture around a computational assumption" means concretely, and it is the honest upper bound of what a structural proof can do for ECDSA-mediated anchoring.

### 4.5 What is not claimed

No lane proves anything about ECDSA soundness, SHA-256 collision resistance, JSON canonicalization across languages, or the Python itself (model-of, not verification-of — §4 preamble; design §7.4 item 4 lists the remainder as "ECDSA soundness + JSON/SHA-256 instantiation — computational, out of structural reach by honest design"). The float-arithmetic boundary of the ladder's integer-tolerance rule (`3 · minority ≤ n`) is pinned by the oracle's enumeration, not by proof (Ladder.lean header, disclosed). Two theorems' propext+Quot.sound footprint is stated, not smoothed over.

## 6. Implementation notes worth stating

Three engineering decisions generalize beyond this project, each already cited by path:

1. **Encode bounded data as one string, not a list literal.** 28k-tuple literals die in elaborators (any language's); a 337KB digit string plus a trivial decoder survives and stays kernel-checkable (§4.2; `TruthTable.lean` header comment records the lesson).
2. **Split case-with-quantifiers before `decide`.** The oracle's 15-line decoder + per-row `Bool` check makes the 28,080-way conjunction a single `native_decide` target (§4.2, Ladder header "case-split anything with quantifiers"). 
3. **Pin the oracle to shipped artifacts, not to a test harness.** The Lean table is regenerated from the same code path that computes the digest in the release receipt; CI green ⇒ the *published* behavior was re-checked, not the test-fixture behavior (§4, "fidelity rests on two receipts").

Core dependencies: `cryptography>=42`, `httpx>=0.27` — nothing else in the runtime; test extras add only `pyyaml`/`jsonschema`. Small TCB is a feature with a citation path: `pyproject.toml`.

## 7. Limitations and exit criteria

Honest inventory, in descending order of significance:

1. **The standard's own exit criteria are external by nature** (design §8, Phase 3): ① a third party implements an independent verifier from the spec — issue #1, open; the closure kit (`docs/verifier-onboarding.md`: Tier-0 trusted-artifact table, nine byte-level obligations with the traps that bit historically, one-command parity harness, proofs-as-behavioral-spec) is in-tree precisely to make this someone else's shortest possible errand; ② first external production deployment — none yet; ③ ≥10 active third-party watcher manifests — self-dogfooded only (all 11 example watchers are mine). Until ①–③ fill, "standard" means "normative draft with published errata," and this paper says so.
2. **Computational boundary**: ECDSA (P-256/Rekor) and SHA-256 soundness are assumed, never discharged (§4.4, §4.5); a proof-toolchain crypto lane (cryptoProofs/EasyCrypt territory) or permanent TCB disclosure is the fork in the road, and the repo chose disclosure plus the architecture theorems that survive it (A4/A0).
3. **Model-of, not verification-of**: the Lean lanes verify abstract models whose fidelity to Python is pinned by the oracle + source digests *inside the enumerated domain*; outside it, fidelity is inspection-grade (Ladder.lean header, verbatim).
4. **Statistical jury risk**: family-diversity defenses fail the way they are designed against — correlated training lineage; the calibration ledger discounts, W1a supremacy constrains, but no cryptographic statement is possible about independently thinking models (design §7.4 risk 4, honest note quoted).
5. **Single-maintainer bus factor**: sole author. `GOVERNANCE.md` states the target pattern (SARIF/Kubernetes: open standard, operational layer above it, foundation transfer if standard status settles) but there is no second key-holder today — stated because an audit protocol's trust story cannot ignore who can silently *stop*.
6. **Publication hygiene as policy**: numbers carry dates because they will move — the project's convention is amend-forward, never rewrite ("Receipts amended forward rather…", `CHANGELOG.md` §0.5.0 line ~92); this paper inherits it (README/CHANGELOG test-count drift was corrected in-repo by `e01b54e` while this draft was written — that correction is itself the pattern working).
7. **The measurement admits its misses**: 3 canary defect classes went undetected and are published (10/3/0), because a catch-everything measurement is a rigged measurement (design §13; README).
